    How to play

    Objective

    The board has six rows, each containing a family of six symbols. Use the clues to place each family's symbols in the correct order, with every symbol appearing once in its row.

    Place every symbol correctly to prove your wisdom. But beware! One false move will bring your tower of deductions tumbling to the ground.

    Clues

    Example Meaning
    A A large symbol on the board is fixed in its current column.
    A △ The two symbols are in the same column.
    A ↔ △ The symbols are in neighboring columns, in either order.
    A ... △ A is somewhere to the left of △, but not necessarily adjacent.
    A △ II These symbols occupy three consecutive columns, with △ in the middle and A and II on opposite sides. The symbols may appear in the order shown or in reverse order.

    You may click or tap a clue to dismiss it once you have drawn all you can from it. This is optional but allows you to focus on the path to true wisdom.

    Controls

    • Left-click a possibility to choose it.
    • Right-click a possibility to discard it.
    • Choosing or discarding the wrong possibility ends the game.
    • When a row’s remaining possibilities force a choice, it falls into place automatically.

    Chalk Marks

    Hold Control (Option on macOS) while clicking to make tentative chalk marks:

    • Left-click to mark a possibility as chosen.
    • Right-click to mark a possibility as discarded.
    • Chalk marks automatically affect other possibilities in the same row, but are not checked against clues or the solution.

    Touchscreens

    • The Tap controls option allows you to select the action performed by a single tap: choosing versus discarding, and permanently inscribing an action versus sketching a chalk mark.
    • The Expanded tiles option makes it easier to select small tiles, at the expense of requiring multiple taps. Enabling Drag tiles allows you to press to open the expanded tiles and release to select one.

    About Logos

    Logos was inspired by the "Einstein puzzle" game published by Flowix Games (which in turn was inspired by the DOS "Sherlock" game, though I never played that).

    This type of puzzle is known as the Zebra Puzzle and sometimes credited to Albert Einstein, though the veracity of that connection is unclear.

    The Forum typeface is by Denis Masharov and is distributed under the SIL Open Font License. Text on the puzzle tiles is derived from TeX Gyre Pagella Bold 2.501, distributed under the GUST Font License.

    The source code for Logos is available on GitHub.
