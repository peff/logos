    How to play

    Objective

    The board has six rows, each containing a family of six symbols, with each symbol appearing exactly once. The symbols within each row have been scrambled, and you have only a set of mysterious clues to guide you to the correct order.

    Place all of the symbols correctly to prove your wisdom. But beware! One false move will bring your logical edifice tumbling down.

    Clues

    Example Meaning
    A A symbol already chosen on the board is fixed in that column.
    A △ The two symbols are in the same column.
    A ↔ △ The symbols are in neighboring columns, in either order.
    A ... △ A is somewhere to the left of △, but not necessarily adjacent.
    A △ II Three consecutive columns contain these symbols, either left-to-right or right-to-left. △ is in the middle; A and II appear on opposite sides.

    You may click or tap a clue to dismiss it once you have drawn all you can from it. This is optional but allows you to focus on the path to true wisdom.

    Controls

    • Left-click a possibility to choose it.
    • Right-click a possibility to discard it.
    • A false choice or discard ends the game.
    • Forced choices (when only one option remains) are filled in automatically.

    Chalk Marks

    Hold Control (or Option on macOS) while clicking to make chalk marks:

    • Left-click to tentatively choose a possibility.
    • Right-click to tentatively discard a possibility.
    • Chalk marks propagate within a row, but are not checked against clues or the solution.

    Touchscreens

    • The Tap Controls option allows you to select the action performed by a single tap: choosing vs discarding, and permanently inscribing an action versus sketching a chalk mark.
    • The Expand Tile option makes it easier to select small tiles, at the expense of requiring multiple taps.

    About Logos

    Logos was inspired by the "Einstein puzzle" game published by Flowix Games (which in turn was inspired by the DOS "Sherlock" game, though I never played that).

    This type of puzzle is known as the Zebra Puzzle and sometimes credited to Albert Einstein, though the veracity of that connection is unclear.
